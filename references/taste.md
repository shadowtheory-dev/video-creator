# Taste defaults learned from the user's notes

Brand-agnostic craft rules from real review rounds. The project's brand profile and the user's latest notes win over these.

## Story and copy
- Headlines carry the message: reading only the on-screen lines, you understand the product. No teaser or mood lines.
- One line at a time, kinetic, placed per shot (centred, large to one side, giant across the frame, a word where its action happens). Never stacked lockups or chat bubbles; a bottom-parked caption is not the default.
- Every line holds at least max(2 s, words ÷ 3.5 s).
- Copy that "makes no sense" or "sounds odd" gets rewritten, not defended; offer 2–3 alternatives in plain text.

## Picture
- One thing moves at a time: type animates while the picture holds; the picture moves under landed type.
- No jump cuts back to a place already visited; every change of scene is carried by an object (a flip, a light, a push into glass, a morph).
- Grids that show scale never repeat an image, and hand over to the next scene with a move rather than a cut.
- No bounce or overshoot on things that seat; they glide in already moving and stop dead, with a contact light or sound.
- Light behaves like light: world-space pools, a pale core, a saturated body, a coloured rim, isolux rings on the ground, washes on facing surfaces, envelopes not pops, no bloom in empty air, no lens-flare sparkle.
- Transitions shouldn't be flat: smear-snap, slat assembly, echo stacks, rolling captions, morph-don't-cut. Avoid nauseating zooms at large type sizes; a clean punch per word beats a zoom.
- Drawn objects: their own style, not a kit's example; every cable goes somewhere a real one would; no clipping; staggered parts rise toward the back.
- UI never lies flat in iso (a screen on the floor like a tablet): upright on a screen or as a floating pane facing the viewer.
- UI in a film never clips: windows resize and reflow when a panel docks.
- On-screen UI is stylised (hairline structure, brand type), never a pixel replica of a real product or OS.
- Design frame 0: it's the thumbnail.

## Moves that have worked (options, not defaults)
- A typed narrator pill over photography for the hook.
- In a supercut, one bar dead centre narrates and big type is kept for object moments.
- A grid that punches out to show scale (four → sixteen → thirty-six), then flips into the next scene.

## Sound
See `sound.md`: continuous, peaks on events, low then bump back up, subdued ending that rides out.

## Process
- Keep storyboards low-fi while the story moves.
- Fast draft renders while scoping, always with a Discord copy.
- Read prompts and write-ups instead of watching reference videos when they say what happens.
- Options, not decisions: lay out 2–4 with your pick and why; the user decides.
